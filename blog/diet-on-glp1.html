What to Eat on GLP-1 Medications: Complete Nutrition Guide

Maximize your weight loss results with the right foods and meal planning strategies

Starting a GLP-1 medication like semaglutide or tirzepatide is exciting, but one of the most common questions we hear is: "What should I actually eat now?"

The good news? There's no strict "GLP-1 diet" you have to follow. But making smart food choices will help you maximize weight loss, minimize side effects, maintain muscle mass, and feel your best throughout treatment.

2x Patients who prioritize protein maintain twice as much muscle mass during weight loss

Why Diet Matters on GLP-1 Medications

GLP-1 medications work by reducing appetite and slowing digestion. This means you'll naturally eat less, but it also means every bite counts more than ever. When you're eating smaller portions, you need to make sure those portions are packed with the nutrients your body needs.

Key considerations when eating on GLP-1s:

  • Muscle preservation: Without adequate protein, you risk losing muscle along with fat
  • Nutrient density: Fewer calories means you need more nutrition per bite
  • Digestive comfort: Some foods may worsen GLP-1 side effects like nausea
  • Sustained energy: The right foods help you feel energized, not depleted

Protein: Your #1 Priority

If there's one thing to focus on, it's protein. Research shows that without adequate protein intake during weight loss, up to 25% of weight lost can be muscle mass, not fat.

Aim for: 0.7-1 gram of protein per pound of your goal body weight

For a person targeting 150 lbs, that's 105-150 grams of protein daily. Here's what that looks like:

  • 4 oz chicken breast = 35g protein
  • 1 cup Greek yogurt = 17g protein
  • 3 eggs = 18g protein
  • 4 oz salmon = 25g protein
  • 1 cup cottage cheese = 28g protein
  • 1 scoop protein powder = 20-25g protein

Protein Strategy

Start every meal with protein first. When your appetite is reduced, you want to prioritize the most important nutrient before filling up on other foods.

Tips for Managing Smaller Appetites

With reduced hunger, you may find it challenging to eat enough. Here's how to make it work:

1. Eat Smaller, More Frequent Meals

Instead of three large meals, try 4-5 smaller ones spread throughout the day. This prevents overwhelming your slowed digestive system.

2. Choose Nutrient-Dense Options

Every bite should count. Skip empty calories like chips or crackers in favor of foods that deliver vitamins, minerals, and protein.

3. Stay Hydrated (But Smartly)

Drink water between meals rather than with meals. Drinking during eating can make you feel too full to finish your food.

4. Prepare Protein-Rich Snacks

Keep hard-boiled eggs, cheese sticks, or Greek yogurt ready for when you have small windows of appetite.

5. Use Protein Shakes Strategically

On days when eating is particularly difficult, a protein shake can help you meet your goals without forcing solid food.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drink alcohol on GLP-1 medications?
Alcohol is best limited or avoided. It provides empty calories, can worsen nausea, and may increase the risk of low blood sugar. If you do drink, stick to one serving and avoid sugary mixers.
What if I can't eat enough protein?
Protein shakes and powders can help bridge the gap. Look for options with 20-30g protein per serving with minimal added sugar. Fairlife protein shakes and Premier Protein are popular choices.
Should I take any supplements?
A daily multivitamin is a good idea since you're eating less. Many providers also recommend B12 (GLP-1s can reduce absorption) and vitamin D. Always discuss supplements with your healthcare provider.
What about fiber?
Fiber is important but increase it gradually. Too much fiber at once can worsen bloating and constipation, which are common side effects. Aim for 20-25g daily from vegetables, fruits, and whole grains.
Is it okay to skip meals if I'm not hungry?
Try to eat something at regular intervals, even if it's small. Skipping meals entirely can lead to inadequate nutrition and may worsen side effects when you do eat. Focus on protein-rich snacks if full meals feel like too much.
