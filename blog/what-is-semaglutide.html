If you've been researching weight loss options, you've likely heard of semaglutide—the medication behind brand names like Ozempic and Wegovy. It's been called a "game-changer" for weight loss, and clinical data backs that up.

But what exactly is semaglutide? How does it work? And is it right for you?

In this comprehensive guide, we'll break down everything you need to know about this FDA-approved medication, from the science behind how it works to realistic expectations for results.

What Is Semaglutide?

Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist—a class of medications that mimic a natural hormone in your body called glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1).

Originally developed for type 2 diabetes (sold as Ozempic), researchers discovered that patients were also losing significant weight. This led to FDA approval of a higher-dose version specifically for weight management, sold as Wegovy.

Key Fact

Semaglutide was approved by the FDA for chronic weight management in June 2021. It's the first weight-loss medication to show an average weight loss of over 15% in clinical trials.

Today, semaglutide is also available in compounded formulations from specialty pharmacies, often at a fraction of the brand-name cost.

How Does Semaglutide Work?

Semaglutide works through multiple mechanisms that address the biological drivers of overeating and weight gain:

1. Reduces Appetite

GLP-1 acts on receptors in your brain's appetite centers, reducing hunger signals. Most patients describe feeling genuinely satisfied with smaller portions—not deprived.

2. Slows Gastric Emptying

The medication slows how quickly food leaves your stomach, helping you feel full longer after meals. This naturally reduces the urge to snack between meals.

3. Reduces Food "Noise"

Many patients report that the constant mental chatter about food—what to eat next, cravings, obsessive thinking—simply quiets down. This psychological benefit can be as impactful as the physical effects.

4. Improves Blood Sugar Regulation

By enhancing insulin release and reducing glucagon, semaglutide helps stabilize blood sugar levels. This reduces the energy crashes that often trigger cravings.

The landmark STEP 1 trial followed nearly 2,000 adults with obesity. Results showed:

  • Average weight loss of 14.9% of body weight (vs. 2.4% with placebo)
  • One-third of participants lost 20% or more of their body weight
  • Significant improvements in blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar
  • Quality of life improvements across all measured categories

For real-world context: if you weigh 200 lbs, average results would mean losing approximately 30 lbs over about 16 months.

Results Timeline

Most people notice appetite changes within 1-2 weeks. Visible weight loss typically begins around week 4-8 as the dose increases. Maximum weight loss is usually achieved around month 12-16.

Dosing Schedule

Semaglutide is administered as a once-weekly subcutaneous injection. The dose is gradually increased to minimize side effects:

  • Weeks 1-4: 0.25mg weekly (starting dose)
  • Weeks 5-8: 0.5mg weekly
  • Weeks 9-12: 1.0mg weekly
  • Weeks 13-16: 1.7mg weekly
  • Week 17+: 2.4mg weekly (maintenance dose)

This gradual increase allows your body to adjust and significantly reduces the likelihood of gastrointestinal side effects. Learn more in our guide to managing GLP-1 side effects.

Side Effects: What to Expect

Like all medications, semaglutide can cause side effects. The most common are gastrointestinal:

  • Nausea (most common, typically mild and improves over time)
  • Diarrhea or constipation
  • Stomach pain
  • Vomiting (less common)
  • Fatigue (especially during dose increases)

For most people, these side effects are temporary and manageable. Eating smaller meals, staying hydrated, and avoiding greasy foods can help. See our full guide on what to eat on GLP-1 medications.

Important Safety Information

Semaglutide should not be used if you have a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2). Tell your doctor about all medical conditions before starting treatment.

Who's a Good Candidate for Semaglutide?

Semaglutide may be appropriate for adults who:

  • Have a BMI of 30 or greater (obesity), OR
  • Have a BMI of 27 or greater with at least one weight-related health condition (high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol)
  • Have struggled to lose weight with diet and exercise alone
  • Are committed to making lifestyle changes alongside medication

Semaglutide is typically not recommended for:

  • People with a history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • Those with a history of pancreatitis
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding women
  • People with severe gastrointestinal conditions

A licensed physician will evaluate your health history, current medications, and goals to determine if semaglutide is right for you.

Semaglutide vs. Other Options

Wondering how semaglutide compares to other weight loss medications? Here's a quick overview:

  • Semaglutide vs. Tirzepatide: Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) targets two hormones instead of one and shows slightly higher average weight loss. Read our full comparison.
  • Semaglutide vs. Phentermine: Phentermine is a stimulant-based appetite suppressant for short-term use. Semaglutide is approved for long-term use with more sustainable results.
  • Semaglutide vs. Surgery: Bariatric surgery typically produces greater weight loss but carries surgical risks. Semaglutide offers a non-invasive alternative.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much weight can you lose on semaglutide?
Clinical trials show average weight loss of 15% of body weight over 68 weeks. For someone weighing 200 lbs, that's approximately 30 lbs. Some people lose more, some less—individual results vary based on factors like starting weight, diet, exercise, and medication adherence.
How long does it take for semaglutide to work?
Most people notice reduced appetite within the first 1-2 weeks. Visible weight loss typically begins within 4-8 weeks as the dose increases. Maximum weight loss is usually achieved around month 12-16 of treatment.
Is semaglutide safe?
Semaglutide is FDA-approved and has been extensively studied in clinical trials involving thousands of patients. Common side effects are gastrointestinal and typically improve over time. However, it's not recommended for people with certain conditions like medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2. Always consult with a healthcare provider.
Do you have to take semaglutide forever?
Semaglutide is approved for long-term use. Research shows that most people regain weight after stopping the medication. Many patients choose to continue at a maintenance dose, while others use it as a tool to establish healthier habits and then work with their doctor on a transition plan.
Can you drink alcohol on semaglutide?
Moderate alcohol consumption is generally considered safe while taking semaglutide. However, alcohol is high in empty calories and can increase nausea—a common side effect. Many patients find their desire for alcohol naturally decreases while on the medication.

The Bottom Line

Semaglutide represents a genuine breakthrough in medical weight loss. For people who have struggled with weight despite diet and exercise efforts, it offers a science-backed tool that addresses the biological factors driving weight gain.

It's not a magic solution—lifestyle changes remain important—but it can make those changes far more achievable by reducing hunger, cravings, and the constant mental battle with food.

If you're considering semaglutide, the next step is to speak with a healthcare provider who can evaluate whether it's appropriate for your specific situation.
