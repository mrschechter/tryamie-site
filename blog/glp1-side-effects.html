GLP-1 Side Effects: What to Expect and How to Manage Them

📅 December 28, 2025 ⏱️ 7 min read ✍️ Try Amie Medical Team

Starting a GLP-1 medication like semaglutide or tirzepatide? Understanding potential side effects—and how to manage them—will help you have the smoothest experience possible.

The good news: most side effects are temporary, mild, and improve as your body adjusts. Here's what to expect and how to feel your best during treatment.

Common Side Effects

GLP-1 medications work by slowing digestion and affecting appetite signals in the brain. This means most side effects are gastrointestinal (GI):

🤢 Nausea ~40-45% of patients

The most commonly reported side effect, especially during the first few weeks and after dose increases. Usually mild and improves over time.

How to Manage:

  • Eat smaller, more frequent meals
  • Avoid greasy, fatty, or spicy foods
  • Eat slowly and stop when you feel satisfied (not full)
  • Stay hydrated with small sips throughout the day
  • Ginger tea or ginger candies can help
💨 Constipation ~20-25% of patients

Slowed digestion can lead to constipation. This typically improves as your body adjusts.

How to Manage:

  • Increase fiber intake (vegetables, whole grains)
  • Drink plenty of water (aim for 64+ oz daily)
  • Stay physically active—even walking helps
  • Consider a fiber supplement if needed
  • Talk to your doctor about OTC remedies if persistent
😴 Fatigue ~10-15% of patients

Some people experience tiredness, especially during dose increases or if not eating enough.

How to Manage:

  • Ensure you're eating enough protein (at least 60g daily)
  • Don't skip meals, even if appetite is reduced
  • Prioritize quality sleep
  • Light exercise can actually boost energy
🌀 Diarrhea ~15-20% of patients

Less common than constipation, but some patients experience loose stools, particularly early in treatment.

How to Manage:

  • Avoid foods that trigger loose stools for you
  • Stay well hydrated
  • Stick to bland foods during episodes
  • Usually resolves as your body adjusts

When Side Effects Typically Occur

Understanding the timeline helps you prepare:

  • Week 1-2: Initial adjustment. Mild nausea most common.
  • After dose increases: Side effects may temporarily return or intensify for a few days.
  • Week 4-8: Most patients report significant improvement in side effects.
  • Month 2+: Side effects typically minimal for most people.
Good News

The gradual dose escalation is specifically designed to minimize side effects. This is why starting at a low dose and slowly increasing is so important.

Serious Side Effects (Rare)

While rare, some side effects require immediate medical attention:

Contact Your Doctor Immediately If You Experience:
  • Severe, persistent abdominal pain (possible pancreatitis)
  • Severe vomiting that won't stop
  • Signs of allergic reaction (rash, swelling, difficulty breathing)
  • Changes in vision
  • Signs of thyroid issues (lump in neck, hoarseness, trouble swallowing)
  • Signs of kidney problems (decreased urination, swelling in legs)

Tips for the Smoothest Experience

Diet Adjustments

What you eat matters more on GLP-1 medications. Check out our complete nutrition guide for GLP-1, but key tips include:

  • Prioritize protein at every meal
  • Eat vegetables before carbohydrates
  • Avoid large, heavy meals
  • Limit alcohol (it hits harder and adds empty calories)
  • Stay hydrated between meals, not during

Lifestyle Tips

  • Inject at night: Some patients find this helps them sleep through any initial nausea
  • Be consistent: Take your injection on the same day each week
  • Exercise regularly: Helps with constipation, energy, and preserving muscle mass
  • Track your intake: Ensure you're getting enough protein and nutrients

What If Side Effects Don't Improve?

If side effects are significantly impacting your quality of life:

  1. Talk to your provider — Dose adjustments may help
  2. Stay at current dose longer — Sometimes your body needs more time
  3. Try a different medication — Some patients tolerate tirzepatide better than semaglutide, or vice versa
  4. Discuss anti-nausea options — Prescription anti-nausea medications can help during adjustment

The Bottom Line

Side effects are a normal part of starting GLP-1 medications, but they're typically temporary and manageable. The key is going slow, eating right, staying hydrated, and communicating with your healthcare provider.

For most patients, the side effects are a small price to pay for the significant, sustained weight loss these medications provide.
